# CETL — Micro-Grant Application Kit

**Prepared:** August 2026 · For reapplying to Outta Excuses, Yippitydoo, Awesome Foundation, and Galaxy Grants
**Status inputs:** LLC registered ✓ · Story anchors: late ADHD diagnosis, brain fog vs. career, building the first agent

> **Why the first round failed (short version):** these four judge a 2–3 question
> personal pitch, not a business plan. One (Galaxy) is effectively a drawing. One
> (Awesome Foundation) doesn't fund businesses at all — it funds community projects.
> The two merit-judged ones (Outta Excuses, Yippitydoo) score specificity and passion,
> and a formal plan reads cold in a pitch box. This kit fixes the instrument, not the
> business.

## 1. The personal statement (master version, ~230 words)

Use this as the base for every application. It's written in first person, in the CETL
voice — edit anything that doesn't sound like you, because *sounding like you* is the
whole point.

> I was diagnosed with ADHD in my forties — around the same time perimenopause turned
> the volume up on everything. I'm a manager. I lead teams. And there I was, mid-fog in
> meetings I was supposed to be running, wondering if the sharpest part of my career was
> behind me. Nobody warns you that those two things arrive together, and nobody hands
> you a plan for leading through it.
>
> What brought my confidence back wasn't a supplement or a planner. I built something.
> With an AI assistant as my patient, judgment-free tutor, I went from zero coding
> background to building working web pages — and then small AI agents that do real
> tasks for me. Watching something I built actually *run* felt like getting my brain
> back.
>
> Clear Enough To Lead (CETL) is the business I built from that moment: a live,
> cohort-based workgroup that teaches perimenopausal women with ADHD — women leading
> teams at work and everything at home — to build real things, designed for how our
> brains actually work. Short wins. Live co-working. Zero condescension. Plus a podcast
> about leading when your brain and body are doing two hard things at once.
>
> I registered the LLC. I built the curriculum and the tools. Now I'm asking for fuel.

**60-word short version (for tight character limits):**

> Diagnosed with ADHD in my forties, mid-perimenopause, while leading teams — I thought
> my best career years were over. Then I learned to build web pages and AI agents, and
> got my confidence back. CETL teaches midlife women with ADHD to do the same: live
> cohorts, ADHD-friendly design, real shipped projects. LLC registered, curriculum
> built. I need fuel.

## 2. Grant-by-grant strategy

### Outta Excuses — Small Business Empowerment Grant
*$3,000 quarterly (with a year-end bonus award), $15 fee. Judged by Be Intentional
Coaching + advisory board. LLC required — you now qualify cleanly.*

**What they score:** clarity and specificity on how funds strengthen operations, plus
demonstrated need and openness to coaching (a coaching org runs it — say yes to
coaching).

**Use-of-funds answer (specific, operations-framed):**
> $3,000 launches CETL's first paid cohort properly: $1,000 produces and captions
> Module 1 so the course is accessible from day one; $1,000 covers podcast launch
> production (mic, editing, artwork for the three scoped episodes) — the podcast is my
> customer-acquisition channel; $600 funds a year of website and email infrastructure
> for enrollment; $400 covers business insurance. Every dollar converts built-but-
> unlaunched assets into a revenue-generating operation.

**Also say:** you'd actively use the business coaching — reviewers from a coaching
organization notice who wants what they offer.
**Reapply:** next quarterly window. Sources: https://www.outtaexcuses.com/eligibility ; https://grantaura.com/grant/outta-excuses-small-business-grant/

### Yippitydoo — The Big Idea Grant
*$1,000 monthly, $15 fee. Scored 40% vision/originality, 40% enthusiasm, 20% use of
funds.*

**What they score:** nearly half is *enthusiasm*. Write like you talk. Exclamation
points are allowed here. The master statement above, plus:

**The Big Idea (vision, 40%):**
> Coding education exists. ADHD coaching exists. Menopause support exists. Nobody has
> put them together — a tech workgroup built for the millions of women whose ADHD got
> louder in perimenopause, taught by one of them. That's CETL: the first build-real-
> things program designed for how our brains work at this exact stage of life.

**Use of funds (20%):**
> $1,000 = my podcast launch kit ($400 mic and editing for the first three episodes),
> one year of website + email enrollment tools ($350), and captioning for Module 1
> ($250) so the first cohort is accessible from day one.

**Reapply:** monthly — resubmit every month with small refreshes.
Sources: https://www.yippitydoo.com/the-big-idea-grant-empowering-women-entrepreneurs/ ; https://grantaura.com/grant/yippitydoo-big-idea-grant/

### Awesome Foundation — $1,000
*Monthly, free to apply, judged by local/topic chapters. Funds PROJECTS with community
impact — explicitly not personal/business asks.*

**The reframe that makes you fundable:** don't pitch CETL. Pitch a free community
project CETL hosts. Based on your answer, here's the project:

**Project pitch — "Fog-Friendly: AI for Absolute Beginners" (free virtual workshop
series):**
> A free, live, virtual workshop series for midlife women with ADHD — the fastest-
> growing group of late-diagnosed adults — taught by a manager who is one of them.
> Over four friendly sessions, total beginners will use AI assistants (Claude, ChatGPT)
> hands-on, create their first HTML pages, make their first git commits to GitHub, and
> build a tiny AI agent — with live co-working ("body doubling") because that's how
> ADHD brains finish things. $1,000 covers a year of workshop tooling, captioning for
> accessibility, and promotion to reach women who think tech left them behind. Every
> attendee leaves with something she built running on her own screen.

**Chapter targeting:** apply to the **Awesome Without Borders / online-friendly
chapters** (your format is virtual). One application, pick the chapter at submission.
**Honest scope note (keep it):** the series teaches first HTML files and mini-agents —
promise "your first web page," not "build a website."
**Reapply:** monthly; chapters rotate and re-entry is normal.
Sources: https://www.awesomefoundation.org/en/faq ; https://blog.awesomefoundation.org/faq/

### Galaxy Grants (Hidden Star)
*~30-second entry form; winners effectively drawn from eligible entrants. Free.*

**Strategy:** there is nothing to optimize — enter every cycle, spend zero additional
effort, and never pay anyone to "improve" a Galaxy entry. Treat wins as found money.
Source: https://galaxyofstars.org/galaxy-grants/

## 3. Reapply calendar

| Cadence | Grant | Effort per cycle |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly | Yippitydoo ($1K) | 15 min — refresh the pitch |
| Monthly | Awesome Foundation ($1K) | 15 min — project pitch |
| Monthly | HerRise ($1K — from the Funding Opportunities doc) | 15 min |
| Monthly | Amber Grant ($10K — from the Funding Opportunities doc) | 20 min |
| Quarterly | Outta Excuses ($3K) | 30 min |
| Each cycle | Galaxy (drawing) | 30 seconds |

Total: roughly **90 minutes a month** of applications once the first versions exist —
a sustainable habit inside 10 hrs/week, with $1K–$10K shots on goal every month.

**Fee honesty:** Outta Excuses, Yippitydoo, and HerRise charge ~$15 per entry
(~$45/month if you enter all three monthly/quarterly). That's normal for these
specific programs — but never pay more than an entry fee, and never pay anyone who
promises to "guarantee" a grant.

## 4. One more thing that strengthens every application

Your LLC is registered — make sure the plan says so everywhere (updated in Business
Plan v2.1), and reference **built, verifiable assets** (curriculum, agents, podcast
slate, the Portfolio) in every pitch. Micro-grant judges see thousands of "ideas";
"registered, built, launching" is the minority position that wins.
