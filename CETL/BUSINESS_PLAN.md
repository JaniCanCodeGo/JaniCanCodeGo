# Clear Enough To Lead (CETL) — Business Plan

**Founder:** Jani
**Date:** July 2026
**Tagline:** *Leadership, life, and stress management for women who are managing everything anyway.*

> **How this plan was made.** This plan was generated from the CETL repository's own
> project data (curriculum, podcast roadmap, positioning) plus a set of independently
> fact-checked research sources. Every statistic cites a numbered footnote; claims that

## 1. Executive Summary

Clear Enough To Lead (CETL) is a two-track business serving one sharply defined customer:
**the perimenopausal woman with ADHD — typically 40–55, a professional who leads teams at
work and runs a household at home — who is ready to build something for herself.**

- **Track 1 — "Build With Claude" (revenue engine):** a paid, cohort-based workgroup
  taking members from zero coding background to shipped work in four modules: **HTML
  Basics → Localhost Projects → Before Apps → Building Mini Claude Agents.** No CS degree
  required, no gatekeeping, no pretending the fog doesn't happen.
- **Track 2 — the "Clear Enough To Lead" podcast (audience engine):** leadership, life,
  and stress management, hosted by a manager doing all of it while managing ADHD and
  perimenopause. Launch episodes are already scoped: *The Bar Is Clear Enough*, *Nobody
  Told Me This Would Happen At Work*, and *The Burnout Math*. Early on, the podcast's job
  is trust and enrollment, not ad revenue.

**Why this business, and why now:**

1. **The pain is measurable and expensive.** A Mayo Clinic study of 4,440 employed US
   women aged 45–60 found 13.4% reported an adverse work outcome from menopause symptoms
   — an estimated **$1.8 billion/year in lost US work time** ($26.6 billion including
   medical costs).[^1] For women with ADHD it is worse: **94% report their ADHD symptoms
   grew more severe during perimenopause and menopause**.[^3]
2. **Nobody serves the intersection.** Women-focused coding schools, ADHD coaching, and
   menopause femtech all exist separately; none combines them,[^21] and only ~7% of
   femtech startups even address menopause.[^15]
3. **The format matches the brain.** Cohort courses complete at 64.2% vs. 48.2% for
   self-paced ones,[^16] and the accountability structures that drive that gap — live
   sessions, peers, body doubling — are the same supports the ADHD evidence base
   recommends.[^10][^11][^12]
4. **Claude collapses the learning curve.** Teaching "build with Claude" rather than
   "memorize syntax" gets beginners to a shipped artifact in week one — the win-loop an
   ADHD learner needs.

**The model:** premium cohorts ($299 beta rising toward $500–$950, consistent with
cohort-pricing benchmarks[^18][^19]) plus an alumni community membership, with the
podcast feeding the funnel. Founder planning assumption: roughly **$20K–$35K revenue in
Year 1**, growing toward **$100K+ by Year 3**. CETL is deliberately founder-run,
low-overhead, and profitable at small scale — not venture-scale.

## 3. The Problem — Researched Pain Points

### 3.1 Menopause measurably damages careers

- 13.4% of 4,440 employed US women aged 45–60 reported at least one **adverse work
  outcome** due to menopause symptoms; 10.8% missed work in the prior year (median 3
  days).[^1]
- Estimated cost: **$1.8 billion/year in lost US work time; $26.6 billion including
  medical expenses**.[^1][^2]
- The damage scales with severity: women in the highest symptom-severity quartile were
  **15.6× more likely** to report an adverse work outcome than those in the lowest.[^1]
- Attrition is real: in a survey of 4,000 UK women aged 45–55, 10% had left a job over
  menopause symptoms, 14% had cut hours, 8% had not applied for promotion, and 73%
  reported brain fog.[^6]

### 3.2 ADHD makes midlife dramatically harder — and is often found late

- ADHD in girls and women is under-recognized and under-researched, producing late
  diagnosis across the lifespan.[^7] In an ADDitude survey of 2,600+ women, **43% were
  first diagnosed between ages 41–50**, and 61% said ADHD had its greatest life impact
  between 40–59.[^4]
- Hormonal transitions — including perimenopause — **exacerbate ADHD symptoms and mood
  disturbance** through estrogen's interaction with the dopaminergic pathways that
  support executive function.[^7]
- Among 1,500+ women with diagnosed or undiagnosed ADHD: **94% said symptoms grew more
  severe in peri/menopause**; ~70% called brain fog, memory issues, and overwhelm
  "life-altering" in their 40s–50s (vs. 11% for memory in their 20s–30s); 83%
  experienced some ADHD symptoms for the first time during the transition.[^3]
- A 2025 study of 656 women aged 45–60 found **higher ADHD symptom severity correlated
  with worse menopausal difficulties and poorer quality of life** on nearly all measures
  — driven by symptom severity, not diagnosis status, meaning many affected women are
  undiagnosed and unsupported.[^8] The symptom overlap itself (sleep, memory,
  concentration) is documented by ADHD advocacy organizations.[^9]

### 3.3 The learning options this woman is offered don't work for her

- Self-paced courses lose half their students: 48.2% completion vs. 64.2% for
  cohort-based courses across a 32,000-course analysis; the median MOOC completes at
  just 12.6%.[^16]
- Adults with ADHD specifically show weaker engagement and outcomes in **self-guided**
  internet-delivered programs versus guided ones.[^13]
- Women-focused coding education is priced and structured for career-switchers
  (Skillcrush's beginner package runs $1,599–$2,499[^20]) as long self-paced grinds —
  the format the completion data indicts, aimed at a different life stage.[^21]

**The synthesis:** a senior, capable, financially established population hits a wall of
brain fog and executive dysfunction at the peak of their careers, gets diagnosed late or
not at all, and is offered either self-paced content they statistically won't finish or
clinical services that don't build anything. CETL occupies exactly that gap.

### 4.1 Top-down

| ADHD-support software (closest proxy for ADHD-support spend) | $1.9B (2025) → $6.7B by 2033 | Grand View Research[^17] |

The honest framing: the widely quoted "$600B menopause economy by 2030" projection did
not survive independent verification and is not used here.[^15] The defensible story is
better anyway — a quarter of the workforce affected,[^5] billions in quantified cost,[^1]
and almost nobody building for it.[^15]

### 4.2 Bottom-up (what viability actually requires)

CETL does not need a slice of a billion-dollar market; it needs 3–5 cohorts of 10–20
women per year. Funnel math (founder assumption): an audience of 2,000 engaged listeners
and subscribers converting at 2% fills 40 seats annually — full capacity for a
founder-taught program. The engaged ADHD-midlife audience demonstrably exists at that
scale: single publications survey 1,500–2,600+ women in this exact demographic.[^3][^4]

**Current starting point (founder input, July 2026): audience of zero.** That makes
audience-building the critical path, not curriculum. The roadmap in §9 therefore front-
loads a 3–6 month audience runway (podcast launch, guesting, email list) before the beta
cohort, with an explicit gate: **do not launch the beta until the email list clears ~150
subscribers**, which at typical warm-launch conversion fills 8–12 beta seats from people
who already trust the voice.

### 4.3 Ideal customer profile

- Woman, 40–55, perimenopausal or early menopause; ADHD diagnosed (often recently) or
  strongly suspected.
- Leads people — a team at work, a family at home, usually both.
- Can spend $500–$1,500 on professional development; courses around $500 on professional
  topics are commonly employer-reimbursable.[^18]
- The real job-to-be-done: *"Prove to myself my brain still works — and build something
  that's mine."*

## 5. The Solution — and Why It's Evidence-Based

### 5.1 Design choices mapped to evidence

| CETL design choice | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Live cohorts, not self-paced content** | 64.2% vs. 48.2% completion across 32,000+ courses;[^16] Harvard's peer-collaborative online case courses hit 85% completion vs. single-digit MOOC rates of that era[^18] |
| **Body-doubling co-working sessions in every module** | Adults with ADHD completed tasks faster with greater perceived accuracy and sustained attention alongside a human *or AI* body double, and preferred it;[^10] Cleveland Clinic describes body doubling as external structure compensating for executive-function deficits.[^11] Caveat stated honestly: the evidence base is young and some neurodivergent people dislike social presence,[^10] so co-working is offered, never required |
| **Structured accountability, goals, check-ins** | A review of 19 studies found ADHD coaching built on goal-setting, psychoeducation, and accountability improves outcomes; increasingly recommended in multimodal adult ADHD care[^12] |
| **Guided, never self-guided** | RCT evidence: adults with ADHD engage less and do worse in self-guided online interventions[^13] |
| **Claude as the always-available tutor** | Kills the classic beginner failure mode (stuck alone at 9pm); the AI body-double findings suggest "working with Claude" itself supports attention[^10] |
| **One shipped artifact per module** | Each module ends with something real: a live personal page (HTML Basics), a project running on her own machine (Localhost Projects), an interactive project (Before Apps), a working personal agent (Mini Claude Agents) — a visible win-loop rather than delayed gratification |

### 5.2 The offer stack

1. **"Build With Claude" cohort** — core offer. 6–8 weeks, 10–20 seats, two live
   sessions weekly (one teaching, one co-working), the four-module arc above, lifetime
   material access. $299 beta rising to $500–$950 (see §8).
2. **CETL Circle** — alumni community, ~$19–29/month: weekly body-doubling calls,
   show-and-tell, next-project support. The premium-cohort-plus-membership hybrid is a
   standard recommended model.[^19]
3. **The podcast** — free. Launch slate: *The Bar Is Clear Enough* (what good-enough
   leadership looks like while managing your team, brain, and body), *Nobody Told Me
   This Would Happen At Work* (leading through perimenopause symptoms), *The Burnout
   Math* (why 40% today beats 120% today and nothing for three days).
4. **Later (Year 2+):** self-paced starter tier for the waitlist, corporate/ERG
   workshops, 1:1 intensives.

## 8. Revenue Model & Financial Projections

*All projections in this section are founder planning assumptions. The pricing
benchmarks they rest on are cited.*

### 8.1 Pricing rationale

- Live-component cohort programs typically price at **$800–$2,500** (full range
  $500–$5,000+),[^19] and Maven's guidance puts full cohort courses at **$950–$3,500+**,
  with ~$500 recommended for a first-time instructor's short course.[^18]
- Cohorts sustain a premium over self-paced because accountability and community are
  the product[^18] — which is CETL's entire design.
- CETL deliberately prices below career-switch programs ($1,599+ at Skillcrush[^20]):
  the promise is capability and confidence, not job placement.

### 8.2 Three-year sketch (founder assumptions)

Calibrated to the founder's actual constraints: ~10 hours/week, audience starting from
zero, beta cohort targeted 3–6 months out (early 2027). Year 1 is therefore an
audience-building year with two cohorts, not three.

| | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cohorts | 2 (first is beta, ~month 5) | 4 | 5 |
| Average seats | 8–12 | 16 | 18 |
| Average price | $299 beta, then $500 | $650 | $800 |
| Cohort revenue | ~$8–9K | ~$42K | ~$72K |
| Community members (avg, $24/mo) | 8 | 50 | 120 |
| Community revenue | ~$2K | ~$14K | ~$35K |
| Podcast revenue | ~$0 | $2–5K | $5–15K |
| **Total** | **~$10–12K** | **~$58–61K** | **~$112–122K** |

**Podcast monetization reality check:** host-read ads average $18–26 CPM,[^22] which is
immaterial at early download counts; shows under ~5,000 downloads/episode should sell
flat-fee host-read integrations instead,[^23] and niche professional-audience shows earn
roughly 60% higher rates once established.[^24] The plan therefore treats podcast money
as a Year 2–3 bonus and its Year 1 value as enrollment.

### 8.3 Costs

Lean by design: recording gear and hosting (~$1–2K one-time), site/email/community
tooling (~$150–300/mo), cohort platform (Maven's ~10% share[^18] or a flat-fee
alternative), LLC/insurance/accounting (~$1–2K/yr), Claude subscriptions for teaching.
Break-even lands inside the first two cohorts (founder assumption). The binding
constraint is founder time and energy — addressed in §10.

## 9. Operations & Roadmap

Calibrated to ~10 founder hours/week and an audience starting at zero, with the beta
cohort targeted 3–6 months out.

**Phase 0 — audience runway (months 1–4, ~10 hrs/wk):** launch the podcast with the
three scoped episodes and hold a weekly cadence (~4 hrs/wk); finish the four curriculum
modules using the repo's content agent (~4 hrs/wk); landing page + email capture; LLC,
waiver, ToS. **Gate to Phase 1: email list ≥ ~150 and all four modules teachable.**

**Phase 1 — beta cohort (months 4–6):** enroll 8–12 beta students at $299 from the list
and warm network; run the beta (the two weekly live sessions temporarily displace most
podcast production — pre-record episodes in Phase 0 to cover the gap); instrument
attendance, completion, and artifacts shipped; collect testimonials.

**Phase 2 (months 6–12):** revise curriculum from beta learnings; raise price to ~$500;
run cohort 2; open CETL Circle to alumni; start the guest-podcast circuit.

**Phase 3 (months 12–24):** price toward $650–$950 as social proof accumulates;[^18][^19]
evaluate Maven as an added distribution channel once testimonials exist;[^18] pilot one
corporate/ERG workshop; first flat-fee podcast sponsorships.[^23]

**Delivery stack (deliberately minimal for beta):** Zoom for live sessions, a simple
email service for the list and nurture sequence, shared cloud folder for materials —
roughly $30/month, no new tools to learn while teaching. Defer Maven (its ~10% share[^18]
buys distribution a zero-audience launch can't yet use) and defer a dedicated community
platform until CETL Circle has actual alumni; revisit both at cohort 3.

**KPIs:** cohort completion (target ≥70%, above the 64.2% cohort benchmark[^16]);
artifacts shipped per student (target: 4 of 4 modules); email list growth; waitlist→seat
conversion; community churn; podcast downloads per episode.

## 10. Risks & Mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| **Founder is a single point of failure** — and lives the same fog she teaches through | Two sessions/week cap; recorded sessions; async-first materials; peer community absorbs support load; the curriculum's honesty about fog *is* the brand |
| **Body-doubling/coaching evidence is young** — overclaiming invites backlash | Market outcomes ("ship four projects with a group"), never therapy claims; cite evidence with caveats, as this plan does[^10][^12] |
| **AI tooling shifts underneath the curriculum** | Teach durable concepts (structure, servers, what an agent is) with Claude as the current instrument; modules are versioned in-repo and regenerable |
| **Conversion assumptions miss** | Bottom-up model needs only ~40 seats/year; if the ADHD+perimenopause framing proves too narrow, the concentric market (midlife women in leadership wanting tech skills) is one message-tweak away |
| **Copycats** | Speed, founder authenticity, podcast trust asset, compounding community — the niche is visibly empty today[^15][^21] |
| **Health-adjacent confusion** | Education-not-medical-advice disclaimers everywhere; no treatment claims; credentialed guests speak for themselves |
| **Platform dependence** | Own the email list; platforms (Maven, podcast hosts) are channels, never the home |
