# The Forgotten Children — Podcast Show Bible

**Working title:** *The Forgotten Children: Life After the 1994 Crime Bill*
**Host:** Jani
**Tagline:** *They sentenced our parents. Nobody counted us.*

---

## What the show is

A story-first podcast by and for the children of the 1994 Crime Bill era — the kids
whose parents were swept into prison during the largest incarceration build-up in
American history, and who grew up in orphanages, foster homes, grandparents' living
rooms, and courtrooms as a result.

The host is one of them. Her mother was sentenced federally for drug offenses under a
three-strikes provision. She moved through an orphanage, foster care, and kinship care
with her grandmother before returning to her mother — and then spent years fighting a
system whose own statistics said she would end up incarcerated too. Instead she became
a two-time college graduate, a two-time homeowner, and a career public servant.

The show holds two truths at once:

1. **The numbers were stacked against us** — and we name those numbers precisely,
   with sources, every episode.
2. **The numbers are not a destiny** — and every episode ends with what carried
   someone through: a grandmother, a caseworker, a teacher, a therapy modality,
   a stubborn refusal.

## What the show is not

- Not a true-crime show. The crime is never the story; the child is the story.
- Not legal or clinical advice. When episodes touch therapy (EMDR, trauma processing),
  the host states plainly that she is not a therapist and points to licensed help.
- Not a debate show about politics. The 1994 Crime Bill is discussed factually —
  what it did, what it didn't do, what the research actually shows — including the
  parts that complicate the popular narrative (three-strikes laws were state laws,
  some predating the bill; the bill accelerated and funded the build-up rather than
  inventing it).

## Format

- **Length:** 35–50 minutes
- **Cadence:** weekly or biweekly (match to production capacity; biweekly recommended
  at launch)
- **Segments:**
  1. **Cold open (1–2 min):** a single voice — the host or a guest — reading one
     memory. No music under the first line.
  2. **"The Number" (3–5 min):** one sourced statistic, explained in plain language.
     Where the number comes from, what it does and doesn't mean. This is the show's
     credibility engine — never repeat a viral number that can't be sourced.
  3. **The Story (20–30 min):** interview or host narrative.
  4. **"What Carried Me" (5 min):** the protective factor — the person, habit, or
     resource that changed the trajectory.
  5. **Close (2 min):** call to action — share your story (website link + email),
     one healing resource, credits.

## Audience

- Adults (now roughly 30–55) who experienced parental incarceration as children in
  the late 1980s–2000s
- Current caregivers of children with incarcerated parents (grandparents, kinship
  and foster caregivers)
- Social workers, educators, CASA volunteers, reentry professionals
- Listeners of narrative-justice shows (Ear Hustle, 70 Million) looking for the
  children's side of the story

## Voice

First-person, direct, warm, zero pity. The host talks like someone who has read the
research *and* lived it. Statistics are always delivered with their source spoken
aloud ("the Bureau of Justice Statistics counted..."), because the audience has been
lied to by round numbers their whole lives.

## Standards

- Every statistic spoken on air must exist in `research/SOURCES.md` with a citation.
- Guests own their stories: pre-interview consent conversation, right to redact
  names, review of the final cut on request.
- Minors are never interviewed. Stories about childhood are told by the adults who
  lived them.
- Mental-health content always carries the disclaimer: *we are not therapists;
  this is not treatment; here is where to find licensed help.*
