# Episode Outlines — Season 1

Each episode follows the segment structure in `SHOW_BIBLE.md`
(Cold open → The Number → The Story → What Carried Me → Close).
Every statistic must be verified in `research/SOURCES.md` before recording.

---

## Ep. 1 — The Statistics Said I'd Be Her
Host origin story. Full script in `EPISODE_01_SCRIPT.md`.

## Ep. 2 — What the Crime Bill Actually Did
Solo explainer. The 1994 Violent Crime Control and Law Enforcement Act, told
honestly: the federal three-strikes provision, the prison-construction and
truth-in-sentencing grant money, the 100,000 police officers, the end of Pell
grants in prison. What it did NOT do: invent three-strikes (Washington 1993,
California 1994, habitual-offender laws long before). How it enhanced sentencing
in states that already had those laws. The Number: prison population growth
through the 1990s.

## Ep. 3 — Grandma's House
The kinship-care generation. Guest: an adult raised by a grandparent after a
parent's incarceration, or a grandmother who raised grandchildren. The Number:
share of children of incarcerated mothers who went to grandparents vs. foster care.

## Ep. 4 — The Clock: ASFA and the 15 Months
How the 1997 Adoption and Safe Families Act's 15-of-22-months rule interacted with
long sentences — parents who lost parental rights not because of what they did to
their children, but because of how long they were gone. The Number: from SOURCES.md
on termination of parental rights among incarcerated parents.

## Ep. 5 — The Daughters
Women were the fastest-growing prison population of the era, and most incarcerated
mothers were primary caregivers. What happens to a household when the mother is the
one taken. The Number: growth of women's incarceration; children of incarcerated
mothers entering foster care.

## Ep. 6 — The Color of the Sentence
Race and the build-up: incarceration rates by race, the racial breakdown of
three-strikes sentencing where measured (California's data), and what disparity
does to specific neighborhoods and schools. Guest: researcher or advocate
(e.g., from a sentencing-reform organization).

## Ep. 7 — Coming Home Is a Second Sentence
Recidivism and reentry, told through a family. What the rearrest numbers actually
measure, why poverty and recidivism feed each other, and what earnings look like
after prison. The Number: BJS recidivism cohort findings + the Pew earnings data.

## Ep. 8 — The Body Keeps the Case File
Trauma and healing. A licensed EMDR clinician as guest (required — host states
on air she is not a therapist). What EMDR is, what bilateral stimulation is, what
"installing a positive cognition" means, who should NOT self-treat. Points to the
website's Healing page and its disclaimers.

## Ep. 9 — The Error Terms
Panel episode: three adults who beat the prediction — different routes (military,
church, sports, school, a single mentor). What the protective-factor research says,
matched against what they lived.

## Ep. 10 — Counted Right
Listener stories episode, built from Share Your Story submissions (with consent).
Season close: what a real accounting of the forgotten children would look like —
the data nobody collected, and the campaign to collect it.
