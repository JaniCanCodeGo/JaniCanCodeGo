# The Forgotten Children of the 1994 Crime Bill

An advocacy, storytelling, and healing project by Jani — for the people who were
children when the 1994 Violent Crime Control and Law Enforcement Act era took their
parents, and who grew up in orphanages, foster homes, and kinship care as a result.

## The seven pages of the website

1. **Home** — mission and thesis
2. **The Bill** — what the 1994 crime bill actually did, and its honest relationship to three-strikes laws
3. **The Numbers** — sourced statistics: race, gender, parental status, foster care, recidivism, poverty
4. **My Story** — the founder's story, and the odds she was given
5. **Healing** — EMDR explained (with disclaimers), bilateral stimulation link, positive cognitions
6. **Podcast** — the show
7. **Share Your Story** — submissions by email

## Ground rules for all content

- **No unsourced statistics.** Every number traces to `research/SOURCES.md`.
- **No myths.** Where a popular claim is unsupported (e.g., the "70% of children of
  incarcerated parents end up in prison" figure), we say so instead of repeating it.
- **Honest history.** Three-strikes laws were state laws; Washington's predated the
  federal bill and California's passed independently the same year. The 1994 federal
  bill added a federal three-strikes provision and paid states to lengthen sentences.
  We describe amplification, not invention.
- **No clinical claims.** The Healing page is educational, carries a
  we-are-not-therapists disclaimer, and encourages licensed care.
